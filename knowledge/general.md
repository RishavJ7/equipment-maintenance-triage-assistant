# General Maintenance and Priority Guide

## Priority definitions
Critical: safety risk or imminent equipment destruction (smoke, fire, sparks, multiple alarms). Stop equipment and act immediately. High: an alarm limit is exceeded or a safety-related function is affected; inspect within 24 hours. Medium: warning limits exceeded or an intermittent fault; schedule within one week. Low: cosmetic or minor issue with all readings normal; handle at the next planned maintenance.

## Safe work and isolation
Before inspecting any equipment, apply lock out and tag out (LOTO), release stored energy such as pressure or mechanical tension, and verify zero energy state. Only qualified technicians may open electrical enclosures. Report any smoke or burning smell immediately and stop the equipment.

## Evidence and diagnosis practice
Sensor readings show symptoms, not causes. A possible cause becomes a confirmed finding only after a technician inspects the equipment and records the finding. Repeated trips, abnormal noise and rising trends should be recorded in the maintenance history. When two sensors disagree, verify with a calibrated handheld instrument before acting on either reading.

## Root Cause Failure Analysis and hierarchy of control
When diagnosing complex machinery issues, apply the hierarchy of evidence: measured telemetry and deterministic threshold checks supersede operator assumptions. Always preserve failed parts in sealed bags for metallurgical examination. Do not restart equipment if a catastrophic hazard has been flagged until a licensed reliability technician signs off the work order.

## Work order content
A work order should include equipment identifier, problem description, recommended inspection or repair actions, tools and parts, safety notes and a priority with its reason.
